Ethical Civic Sponsorship

Standalone deployment retired

Corporate sponsorship of civic institutions is not ethically neutral. The research on how industry sponsorship shapes institutional behavior, research agendas, and policy positions is three decades deep. Governance is the structural response.

What the module examines

Fooks et al. (2013) documented using internal tobacco industry records how corporations use CSR frameworks to neutralize regulatory threats and build civic relationships — making civic institutions participants in their neutralization strategy regardless of intent. Fabbri et al. (2018) found in a scoping review of 36 articles that industry sponsorship consistently drives research agendas away from public-health-relevant questions. Lundh et al.'s Cochrane review (2017) confirmed that industry-sponsored drug and device studies are more likely to report favorable outcomes. The governance failure is that most institutions accept sponsorship without a written policy, a mission alignment review, documented exclusion criteria, or an independent review body.

M6 sits in the Legal & Justice group of the framework.

What the assessment measured

The module organised a structured self-assessment across six governance dimensions:

  1. 1Sponsorship Ethics Policy
  2. 2Mission Alignment Review
  3. 3Exclusion Criteria
  4. 4Transparency Standards
  5. 5Conflict of Interest Management
  6. 6Review & Accountability Mechanism

Each dimension was described against tiers running from early stage up to the fully implemented tier the framework calls PIONEERING, with observable criteria written for each level, so that an institution could locate its own arrangements rather than receive a score. It was a self-assessment framework for institutional reflection, and never a validated instrument, an audit, an accreditation, or a compliance determination.

Who it was written for

Nonprofit organizations · Community health organizations · Academic institutions · Civic event producers · Research institutions · Foundation-funded programs

And anyone who trusts these institutions — donors, members, patients, and the public. Sponsorship you never see shapes what an institution does on your behalf; this keeps it accountable to you.

Why the standalone deployment was retired

The fifteen modules were first published as fifteen separate deployments. Scope, evidence, and release status then had to be maintained in fifteen places, and they drifted apart. The hub now holds one canonical page per module, and these fifteen addresses point at it.

Assessment collection is paused across all fifteen modules. The published operations matrix records the same position for every one of them: the canonical route is reachable, collection is paused, and advisory work is delivered by a person rather than by automated scoring. This page is a static record. It carries no forms and collects nothing.
